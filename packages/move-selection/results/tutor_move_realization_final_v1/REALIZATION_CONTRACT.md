# Final four-move realization contract

This contract governs move-to-language realization only. Turn-LinTS selection, probabilities, posterior sampling, context, reward, and updates are unchanged.

## Generic

- Purpose: Support, acknowledge, orient, or transition without diagnosing or teaching the missing method.
- Questions: Optional and uncommon; never automatically diagnostic.
- Hint/detail: Broad orientation only; no targeted corrective hint or worked step.
- Explanation: Low: do not explain the solution method.
- Length: Usually 1-3 useful sentences.

PRIMARY SPEECH ACT: context-aware support, acknowledgement, orientation, or
transition. Produce a useful, non-vacuous statement tied to the current problem
or the learner's progress. Usually use 1-3 concise-to-moderate sentences. Do not
target a specific reasoning gap, introduce a corrective hint, explain the
missing method, or reveal a worked step. Do not default to interrogating the
learner; a question is optional and must not turn generic into probing. Use the
dialogue history to avoid repeating stock phrases such as "Great", "Okay", or
"Let's continue", including when generic is selected on consecutive turns.

## Probing

- Purpose: Elicit the learner's mathematical reasoning, interpretation, or chosen method.
- Questions: One clear, specific main reasoning question.
- Hint/detail: Minimal setup; the learner supplies the important step.
- Explanation: Low: never answer the probe before asking it.
- Length: Usually 1-3 sentences.

PRIMARY SPEECH ACT: elicit learner reasoning, interpretation, recall, or chosen
method. Usually use 1-3 sentences with ONE clear, specific main mathematical
reasoning question; one short setup sentence is allowed. The learner must
supply the important step. Do not answer the question, work the solution, or
explain the missing method before asking. Avoid vague stock questions and
several unrelated or sequential questions.

## Focus

- Purpose: Direct attention to the specific relation, error, quantity, representation, or next substep that matters.
- Questions: Not required; any question is secondary to the targeted cue.
- Hint/detail: Targeted and more direct than probing, while leaving meaningful work.
- Explanation: Medium-low: identify what matters without fully working it out.
- Length: Usually 2-4 sentences when explanation is useful.

PRIMARY SPEECH ACT: direct attention to ONE specific mathematical relation,
error, quantity, representation, clue, or immediate sub-step. Give a targeted
cue, observation, or directive that is more direct than probing but less
explicit than telling. Usually use 2-4 sentences when explanation is useful,
and leave meaningful mathematical work for the learner. It does not have to be
a question; any short check is secondary to the cue. Do not fully work out the
answer, become a diagnostic interview, or collapse into generic encouragement.

## Telling

- Purpose: Explicitly teach the relevant fact, method, explanation, representation, or worked step.
- Questions: Optional only after the instruction or explanation.
- Hint/detail: Most direct and instructional; equations are encouraged when helpful.
- Explanation: High: supply and connect the needed mathematical information.
- Length: Usually 2-5 sentences; slightly longer only when clarity requires it.

PRIMARY SPEECH ACT: explicitly teach the relevant fact, method, representation,
explanation, missing step, or worked clarification. Put understandable
mathematical information FIRST, connect it to the current problem, and use
equations or step-by-step wording when helpful. Usually use 2-5 sentences; a
slightly longer response is allowed only when clarity requires it. Do not be
terse, disguise telling as leading questions, or ask what to do without first
supplying the needed instruction. A short invitation may appear only after the
teaching or explanation.
