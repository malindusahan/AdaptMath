# Manual move-realization review cases

These 12 deterministic review cases are prompts/checklists, not generated Tutor outputs and not claims that every listed move is equally appropriate. Cases 1-4 deliberately hold the learner state fixed to make speech-act differences visible.

## Shared misconception: multiplying two negatives

Problem: Simplify `(-3)(-5) + 2`.

Learner history: The learner says, "Negative times negative is negative, so I got -13."

### Case 1 — Generic

Desired characteristics: acknowledge the attempt neutrally; remain supportive and primarily declarative; do not identify the sign error, explain the sign rule, or automatically ask for reasoning.

### Case 2 — Probing

Desired characteristics: ask one specific diagnostic question about the sign of a product of two negative numbers; do not state the sign rule first.

### Case 3 — Focus

Desired characteristics: direct attention specifically to the sign of `(-3)(-5)` with a cue or directive; do not give the completed simplification and do not turn the primary act into a broad question.

### Case 4 — Telling

Desired characteristics: state and briefly explain that multiplying two negative numbers gives a positive product before any optional follow-up; do not use a chain of leading questions.

## Case 5 — Generic transition after correct progress

Problem: Solve `3x + 7 = 22`.

Learner history: The learner correctly subtracts 7 and obtains `3x = 15`.

Selected move: generic.

Desired characteristics: concise acknowledgement/encouragement that does not teach division, target a gap, or demand another explanation by default.

## Case 6 — Probing an unsupported operation

Problem: A rectangle has area 48 and width 6. Find its length.

Learner history: The learner says, "I think I should add 48 and 6."

Selected move: probing.

Desired characteristics: one context-specific question eliciting why addition would recover a missing factor; do not reveal division before asking.

## Case 7 — Focus on a fraction relationship

Problem: Compare `3/4` and `5/8`.

Learner history: The learner compares only the numerators and chooses `5/8`.

Selected move: focus.

Desired characteristics: direct attention to using a common denominator or equivalent representation; narrow attention without completing both conversions or merely asking "What do you think?"

## Case 8 — Telling a missing equation step

Problem: Solve `2(x - 3) = 10`.

Learner history: The learner says they do not know how to begin and explicitly asks for the first step.

Selected move: telling.

Desired characteristics: explicitly provide one valid first step and why it works; explanation comes before any invitation to continue; do not give the entire remaining solution.

## Case 9 — Generic after self-correction

Problem: Evaluate `18 - 7 + 4`.

Learner history: The learner catches an earlier arithmetic mistake and corrects `15` to `11` before continuing.

Selected move: generic.

Desired characteristics: neutral acknowledgement of the self-correction and a natural transition; no corrective hint, method explanation, or routine interrogation.

## Case 10 — Probing proportional reasoning

Problem: Four notebooks cost $12. What do seven notebooks cost at the same rate?

Learner history: The learner writes `12 + 7`.

Selected move: probing.

Desired characteristics: one diagnostic question about what $12 represents relative to four notebooks; do not supply the unit rate first.

## Case 11 — Focus on equation balance

Problem: Solve `x/5 + 2 = 9`.

Learner history: The learner subtracts 2 only from the left side.

Selected move: focus.

Desired characteristics: targeted declarative cue about preserving equality by applying an operation to both sides; do not complete the equation or broaden into general Socratic questioning.

## Case 12 — Telling after repeated non-engagement

Problem: Find 25% of 80.

Learner history: Two consecutive messages say the learner cannot proceed and wants direct help.

Selected move: telling.

Desired characteristics: directly explain one concise method and the needed calculation; instruction first; a follow-up question is not required merely to prolong dialogue.

## Reviewer checklist

- Can the primary speech act be inferred without seeing the internal label?
- Is generic supportive rather than a disguised probe?
- Is probing specific and genuinely diagnostic?
- Is focus a targeted cue rather than an open-ended probe?
- Does telling actually provide information before any optional question?
- Is the turn atomic, correct, concise, grounded, and free of learner-visible internal metadata?
