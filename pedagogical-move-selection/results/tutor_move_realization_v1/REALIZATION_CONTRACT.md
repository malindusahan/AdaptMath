# Tutor move realization contract v1

The externally selected pedagogical move is authoritative. This contract changes only the primary learner-facing speech act; it never changes move selection or arm availability. Raw internal move labels must not be shown to the learner.

## Generic

Primary speech act: neutral acknowledgement, encouragement, transition, or broadly supportive response.

- Prefer declarative/supportive language.
- Do not target a specific reasoning gap.
- Do not introduce a corrective hint or explain the solution method.
- Do not default to interrogating the learner.
- A question is optional only when genuinely necessary; generic must not become probing by default.

## Probing

Primary speech act: elicit learner reasoning, recall, explanation, or thinking.

- Normally ask one clear, context-specific diagnostic or reasoning question.
- Leave the cognitive work with the learner.
- Do not provide the missing step or explain the method before the probe.
- Avoid vague stock questions when a specific probe is possible.

## Focus

Primary speech act: direct attention to one specific error, relationship, clue, representation, or sub-step.

- Use a targeted cue, observation, or directive.
- Narrow the search space without supplying the full explanation or answer.
- Do not automatically rewrite the cue as an open-ended question.
- A short check may follow only when the targeted cue remains primary.

## Telling

Primary speech act: provide explicit instruction, explanation, method, missing step, or worked clarification.

- Put the explanation or instruction first and actually supply the information.
- Do not disguise telling as leading questions.
- Give only the needed fact/step when a whole solution would be unnecessary.
- A short follow-up invitation may occur after the explanation, but the response must remain unmistakably instructional.

## Universal requirements

Every move remains concise, mathematically correct, relevant, natural, age-appropriate, non-repetitive, and grounded in the problem/history. There is no universal requirement to ask or end with a question. The Tutor must not expose selector names, probabilities, BKT, LinTS, MRB1, or internal move labels.
